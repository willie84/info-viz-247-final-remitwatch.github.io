The Scale of the Problem

Amina's missing money isn't a glitch.
It's a global system.

You've seen how the fees work. Now see how they play out across millions of real transfers — in Amina's corridor, across Africa, and globally. The World Bank has been tracking it for years.

01 / The Providers

Banks charge 3× more than mobile apps.

Just like SendFree hid its fee in a bad exchange rate, traditional commercial banks average 8.3% globally. Mobile operators sit near 3.1% — the only provider type actually meeting the UN's 3% target.

02 / Amina's Route

Brand names mean higher fees.

We used fictional apps on page one — but here is the real UK → Kenya data. Transparent apps cost around 1%. Legacy giants like Western Union sit over 8%. Same route. Same destination. Wildly different outcomes for Amina's mother.

03 / The Global Toll

Sub-Saharan Africa pays the highest price on earth.

The UN target is 3%. Sub-Saharan Africa averages 11.5%. Every other region in the world is closer to the target than Africa. This system drains capital directly from the communities that need it most.

04 / The Corridor Problem

Where you send from dictates the price.

Amina sends from London — one of the cheaper routes into Kenya at 4.9%. But Tanzania → Kenya, an intra-African corridor, averages 20.98%. The assumption that sending within Africa is cheaper is simply wrong.
